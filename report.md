
# Comprehensive Report: 2026 Iran-Israel War (March Updates)

## Overview
A major regional war began on 28 February 2026 when the United States and Israel launched surprise airstrikes on multiple sites and cities across Iran. The initial strikes resulted in the assassination of Supreme Leader Ali Khamenei and several other high-ranking Iranian officials. The attack, launched during indirect nuclear negotiations, prompted immediate and widespread retaliation from Iran, which launched hundreds of drones and ballistic missiles at targets in Israel and at US military bases across the Middle East.

The war has caused severe global economic impacts, described as the world's largest supply disruption since the 1970s energy crisis, surging oil and gas prices, and widespread disruptions in aviation, tourism, and financial markets.

## Latest Developments (Published On or After 23 March 2026)

### Key Diplomatic & Military Developments (March 23-27)
*   **Failed Ceasefire Push:** Following an ultimatum from US President Donald Trump warning Iran to reopen the Strait of Hormuz or face strikes on its power grid, the US extended a "five-day pause" on strikes on Iranian power plants to allow for diplomatic talks. However, Iran firmly rejected claims of "productive" negotiations, stating it would not end its campaign until its demands (and reparations) were met. It further threatened to heavily mine the Persian Gulf in the event of a land invasion.
*   **Unrelenting Strikes:** Israel launched massive and continuous waves of airstrikes against Iranian infrastructure. By March 25, Russia's RIA Novosti reported that Israel had dropped over 15,000 munitions on Iran since the start of the conflict. In turn, Iran maintained its missile barrages. Between February 28 and March 25, Israel estimated Iran had fired around 470 missiles into its territory. The cost of the US operation against Iran is now estimated to exceed $30 billion.
*   **Targeting Nuclear and Energy Sites:** US and Israeli strikes heavily targeted areas tied to Iran's nuclear infrastructure and vital economic sites, severely threatening the remainder of its nuclear program. Both nations also targeted energy infrastructure, with Iran claiming successful strikes on naval and fuel facilities in Haifa, Israel.

### Status of India (26-27 March 2026)
India finds itself in an increasingly precarious geopolitical and economic position due to the escalating conflict.
*   **Economic Impact and Energy Crisis:** The prolonged closure of the Strait of Hormuz has severely disrupted India's energy imports. This has led to a major domestic LPG crisis and skyrocketing fuel prices. However, on March 26, Iranian Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi announced that Iran would allow India (along with Pakistan, Spain, and Germany) to safely use the Strait of Hormuz route, offering a crucial lifeline. Following this announcement and hopes for a ceasefire, global oil prices temporarily dropped by 5%.
*   **Diplomatic Stance and Domestic Politics:** Prime Minister Narendra Modi publicly called for peace and conflict resolution through dialogue and diplomacy, firmly stating that disruptions in the Strait of Hormuz are "unacceptable." However, India's perceived inaction has drawn intense domestic criticism. Opposition figures, including the CPI(M) and Congress leaders Jairam Ramesh and Akhilesh Prasad Singh, severely slammed the government's stance, criticizing Modi for failing to explicitly condemn the US and Israel in Parliament.
*   **The Pakistan Factor:** Much to New Delhi's dismay, Pakistan has actively stepped into the role of mediator between the US and Iran. This development has put PM Modi "in the hot seat" and led to intense backlash against External Affairs Minister S. Jaishankar over remarks made regarding Pakistan's mediation efforts. Meanwhile, Jaishankar met with the Iranian envoy to express appreciation for Iran ensuring the safety and support of Indians currently residing in Tehran.
